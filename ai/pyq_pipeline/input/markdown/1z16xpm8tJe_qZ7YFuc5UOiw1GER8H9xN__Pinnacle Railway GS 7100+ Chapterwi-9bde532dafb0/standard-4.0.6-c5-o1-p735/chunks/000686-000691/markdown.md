(a) Conveying motor commands from the brain to muscles

(b) Integrating reflex responses at the spinal cord level only

(c) Transmitting sensory information from receptors to the central nervous system

(d) Facilitating secretion of

**Sol.590.(c)** Afferent neurons, also known as sensory neurons, carry signals from sensory receptors such as skin, eyes, ears, and internal organs towards the brain and spinal cord. They enable the central nervous system to perceive stimuli like touch, pain, temperature, and sound. In contrast, efferent neurons transmit motor commands from the CNS to muscles and glands. Thus, afferent neurons are crucial for detecting environmental and internal changes and relaying this information for processing and response.
