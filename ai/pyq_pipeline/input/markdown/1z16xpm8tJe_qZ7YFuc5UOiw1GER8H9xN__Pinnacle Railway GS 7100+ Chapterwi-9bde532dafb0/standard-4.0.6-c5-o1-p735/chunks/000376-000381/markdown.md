**Q.616.** What is the primary environmental impact of the release of freon from refrigeration plants on the atmosphere? NTPC CBT - I (GL) 20/06/2025 (Morning) (a) Stratospheric ozone depletion (b) Increased oxygen level (c) Increased nitrogen (d) Reduced greenhouse gas emissions

**Sol.616.(a) Stratospheric ozone depletion.** Freon, a type of chlorofluorocarbon (CFC) used in refrigeration and air-conditioning, is chemically stable in the lower atmosphere but rises to the stratosphere, where ultraviolet (UV) radiation breaks it down. This releases chlorine atoms, which catalytically destroy ozone molecules, leading to stratospheric ozone depletion. The thinning of the ozone layer increases UV radiation reaching the Earth, causing health hazards like skin cancer and ecological damage.

(a) Mass movement would be more frequent (b) Soil fertility would improve (c) Erosion would be insignificant (d) Erosion would increase

**Sol.620.(c) Erosion would be insignificant.** Weathering breaks down rocks into smaller fragments through physical, chemical, or biological processes. These fragments enable erosion—transport by water, wind, or ice—so without weathering, erosion would be minimal. Weathering is the first step in the rock cycle leading to soil formation and sediment transport. It directly influences landform development and geomorphic processes. Without weathering, rock surfaces would remain intact, making mass movement less likely. Fertile soil formation depends on weathered rock particles, so soil fertility would not improve without weathering.
