**Q.697.** Which of the following factors best explains the formation of ox-bow lakes along river courses?

NTPC CBT - I (UG) 07/08/2025 (Afternoon)

(a) Sudden uplift of riverbeds by tectonic activity

(b) Tectonic uplift disrupting the flow

(c) Glacial retreat leaving depressions

(d) Lateral erosion on the outer banks and deposition on the inner banks

**Sol.697.(d)** Ox-bow lakes form in the middle and lower courses of rivers where the flow is gentle and meanders develop. Lateral erosion on the outer bank of a meander bend and deposition on the inner bank cause the meanders to grow closer together. Eventually, the neck between two adjacent bends narrows and is cut off during floods, creating an ox-bow lake (a U-shaped lake). Over time, sediment seals the ends, leaving the meander loop isolated. Ox-bow lakes are classic features of fluvial geomorphology and are indicators of river maturity and lateral erosion processes.
